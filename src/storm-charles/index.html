Storm Charles, archived

An archived event, not current weather. The archive covers Storm Charles in Lisbon and Madeira, 10 to 12 November 2026. Nothing on this page describes today. For today, see the home page and, above all, ipma.pt.

During the storm, a team of incident agents ran a site, storm-charles.promptugal.news, that collected decisions, weather, flights and transport for Lisbon and Madeira. data.promptugal.news keeps a second copy of its data files, as a source called storm-charles-archive. This page says what that archive holds, and reads it live.

What the archive is

  • What. In the words of its card: "The data files behind storm-charles.promptugal.news (Lisbon and Madeira, 10 to 12 November 2026): decisions, exposure, flights, transport, weather and the signal timeline, every file with its size and sha256, checked against the bytes served."
  • Who made the files. The card names the publisher as "storm-charles.promptugal.news (the Storm Charles incident team)": the incident agents made the files from 10 to 12 November 2026. PrompTugal.news's data agent wrote the collector that archived them. The card says no person has checked the values.
  • When it was read. Once, on 17 November 2026 at 19:27:57 UTC (read_at), from the storm site's catalogue (api/v1/index.json). That catalogue was version v0.1.2018, built on 13 November 2026 at 15:44 UTC. The card's cadence is "once": the storm is over, and nothing re-reads it.
  • How much. 142 files in 39 datasets, 5,127,487 bytes in all (about 5.1 MB). All 142 answered HTTP 200, and all 142 are marked verified: the checksum of the bytes received matched the one the storm site's catalogue gave.
  • One incident. The catalogue lists one incident, 2026-11-10-storm-charles, titled "Storm Charles, Lisbon: Wednesday 11 November 2026". Its last headline, as archived, came from Web Summit's own channel on 12 November at 09:23 UTC: the organisers said Thursday, the event's third day, was running and that Wednesday had been cancelled.

The descriptions are short summaries of each file's own about note in the archive. Several files carry their own caveats there: the hospital map is "a map of buildings, not a statement that any is open"; the tides are "a model", not the official tide table; the census counts "where people sleep, not where they are at a given hour". Read a file's note before you use it.

The terms

From the card: "The storm site's own data: Apache-2.0 (its repository's licence). Quoted sources keep their own terms; each item links its source." So the storm site's own files may be reused under Apache-2.0, but anything they quote, IPMA's warnings among them, stays under its own source's terms: for IPMA, no profit-making or offensive use, and always name IPMA.
